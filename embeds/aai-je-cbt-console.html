Part A · common to all disciplines

A1

Reasoning

high-yield

Pure speed marks. The trick is pattern recognition — spot the type in 3 seconds (series / coding / relations / direction / syllogism) and apply the fixed method.

▸Number series

Check first differences; if not constant, check second differences, then ratios (×/÷), then squares/cubes/primes. Most series fall to one of these.

▸Blood relations

Draw a quick tree. "My father's only son" = the speaker (if male). Convert every clause to +/− generation and male/female before answering.

▸Directions

Sketch it. Each right turn cycles N→E→S→W; each left N→W→S→E. Net displacement = combine along the two axes; distance = Pythagoras if needed.

Q1
Find the next term: 3, 6, 11, 18, 27, ?
1
First differences: 6−3=3, 11−6=5, 18−11=7, 27−18=9 → the gaps are 3,5,7,9 (consecutive odd numbers).
2
Next gap = 11 → 27 + 11.
ans 38
Q2
Pointing to a photo, a man says, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is she related to him?
1
"My grandfather's only son" = the man's father.
2
So she is "the daughter of my father" = the man's sister.
ans She is his sister
Q3
A man walks 5 km North, turns right and walks 3 km, turns right again and walks 5 km. How far and in which direction is he from the start?
1
North 5. First right (facing N) → East 3. Second right (facing E) → South 5.
2
North 5 and South 5 cancel. Only East 3 remains.
ans 3 km, due East of the start
Q4
In a code, MANGO → NBOHP. Encode FRUIT.
1
Each letter shifts +1: M→N, A→B, N→O, G→H, O→P. It's a simple forward shift.
2
Apply +1 to FRUIT: F→G, R→S, U→V, I→J, T→U.
ans GSVJU
Q5
Statements: All pens are books. All books are pencils. Which conclusion follows?
1
Chain the universals: pens ⊆ books ⊆ pencils.
2
Therefore pens ⊆ pencils. (The reverse, "all pencils are pens", does NOT follow.)
ans All pens are pencils — valid
A2

Quantitative Aptitude

practice daily

The single most improvable section. Percentages, ratios, profit-loss, time-speed-distance, time-work, SI/CI — learn the shortcut form of each and you'll clear these in under a minute.

▸Successive % change

For +a% then +b%, net = a + b + ab/100 (use −ve for decrease). +20 then −20 → 20−20−400/100 = −4%. Never just add.

▸Speed conversions

m/s → km/h: ×18/5. km/h → m/s: ×5/18. Crossing a pole: time = length ÷ speed. Crossing a platform: add platform length.

▸Work = LCM trick

Take total work = LCM of the days. Each worker's rate = total ÷ their days. Add rates, divide total by the sum. Fast and no fractions.

Q1
A number is increased by 20% then decreased by 20%. Net change?
1
Multiply factors: ×1.20 then ×0.80 = 0.96.
2
0.96 means 96% of the original → a 4% drop.
ans 4% decrease (not 0% — the shortcut a+b+ab/100 confirms it)
Q2
A 120 m long train crosses a pole in 6 seconds. Find its speed in km/h.
1
Crossing a pole → distance = train length = 120 m. Speed = 120 ÷ 6 = 20 m/s.
2
Convert: 20 × 18/5 = 72.
ans 72 km/h
Q3
A can finish a job in 10 days, B in 15 days. Working together, how long?
1
Let total work = LCM(10,15) = 30 units. A's rate = 3/day, B's rate = 2/day.
2
Combined = 5 units/day → 30 ÷ 5 = 6.
ans 6 days
Q4
Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹5000 at 10% per annum for 2 years.
1
SI = P·r·t/100 = 5000·10·2/100 = ₹1000.
2
CI = P[(1+r/100)² − 1] = 5000(1.21 − 1) = ₹1050.
3
Shortcut for 2 years: difference = P·(r/100)² = 5000·(0.1)² = 5000·0.01.
ans Difference = ₹50  (SI ₹1000 · CI ₹1050)
Q5
The average of 5 numbers is 20. If a number 18 is removed, what is the average of the rest?
1
Total = 5 × 20 = 100. Remove 18 → remaining sum = 82 over 4 numbers.
2
82 ÷ 4 = 20.5.
ans 20.5
Q6
The ages of A and B are in ratio 3:5 and their sum is 40. Find each age.
1
Let ages be 3x and 5x → 3x + 5x = 8x = 40 → x = 5.
2
A = 3×5 = 15, B = 5×5 = 25.
ans A = 15 years, B = 25 years
A3

English Comprehension

quick wins

Grammar rules, error spotting, vocabulary and reading comprehension. The grammar rules are finite — nail the top ones and error-spotting becomes automatic.

▸"One of the ___"

one of the + plural noun + singular verb. "One of the best players is…" A classic error-spotting trap.

▸since vs for

since + a point in time (since 2015, since Monday). for + a duration (for 3 years). Both pair with present perfect / perfect continuous.

▸RC time-saver

Read the questions first, then scan the passage for keywords. Answer vocabulary/detail questions before inference/tone ones — they're faster and safer.

Q1
Spot the error: "He is one of the best player in the team."
1
"One of the" must be followed by a plural noun.
2
"player" → "players".
ans Correct form: "one of the best players in the team"
Q2
Fill in the blank: "She has been working here ___ 2015."
1
2015 is a fixed point in time, not a duration → use since.
ans since
Q3
Choose the synonym of EPHEMERAL.
1
Ephemeral = lasting a very short time. Root hint: "ephemera" = things that don't last.
ans Transient / short-lived (opposite: permanent, lasting)
A4

General Awareness

aviation edge

Static GK + current affairs. Your unfair advantage here is AAI & aviation GK — very likely to appear in an AAI paper and easy to lock down. These facts are verified below.

◆AAI — the essentials

Formed 1 April 1995 by merging the International Airports Authority of India (1972) and the National Airports Authority (1986), under the Airports Authority of India Act, 1994.

It's a statutory body / Miniratna Category-1 PSE under the Ministry of Civil Aviation.

◆AAI — where & what

HQ: Rajiv Gandhi Bhawan, Safdarjung Airport, New Delhi. Five regional HQs: Delhi (N), Mumbai (W), Kolkata (E), Chennai (S), Guwahati (NE).

Role: manages 130+ airports and provides CNS/ATM (air traffic management) over Indian airspace.

◆Aviation bodies — don't mix up

AAI = operator/infrastructure. DGCA = safety regulator. BCAS = civil aviation security.

International: ICAO (UN body, HQ Montreal, from the 1944 Chicago Convention) & IATA (airline trade body, HQ Montreal).

▸Static GK to prioritise

Indian polity basics, geography (rivers, states, capitals), key dates/history, science-everyday, economy terms, sports, awards, books & authors. Build a one-page cheat-sheet and revise weekly.

▸Current affairs window

Focus on the last 6–8 months before the exam: national schemes, appointments, summits, defence, sports events, and anything aviation/airport in the news. Skim a monthly capsule.

✕Don't over-memorise volatile facts

Post-holders and "latest chairman/CEO" change often. Verify names close to your exam date from a current source rather than trusting an old list.

Q1
The Airports Authority of India was formed in which year, and under which ministry?
1
Formed on 1 April 1995 by merging IAAI and NAA under the AAI Act, 1994.
2
It functions under the Ministry of Civil Aviation.
ans 1995 · Ministry of Civil Aviation
Q2
Which body is the safety regulator of Indian civil aviation (not the airport operator)?
1
AAI operates airports/airspace; regulation of safety is a separate body.
2
That regulator is the DGCA — Directorate General of Civil Aviation. (Security is BCAS.)
ans DGCA (Directorate General of Civil Aviation)
Q3
Where is the headquarters of ICAO, and what does it govern?
1
ICAO = International Civil Aviation Organization, a specialised UN agency from the 1944 Chicago Convention.
2
It sets global standards for civil aviation; HQ in Montreal, Canada.
ans Montreal, Canada — sets international civil-aviation standards

B-Q1
Time complexity of for(i=1;i<n;i=i*2) sum++; ?
1
i doubles each iteration: 1, 2, 4, 8, … It stops once i ≥ n.
2
Number of doublings to reach n is log₂n.
ans O(log n)
B-Q2
Relation R(A,B,C), FDs A→B, B→C. Find the candidate key and highest normal form.
1
A⁺ = {A} → +B (A→B) → +C (B→C) = all attributes → A is the candidate key.
2
Single-attribute key → in 2NF (no partial dependency possible).
3
But A→B→C is a transitive dependency (B is not a superkey, C is non-prime) → violates 3NF.
ans Candidate key = A · highest normal form = 2NF
